Canonical news workflow review

26 October 2026. PR23 · News reader · Delivered release manifest.

What changed and why

One installed news application replaces the abandoned MVP and agent prototypes. Ingestion, article analysis, grouping, editorial policy, synthesis, PostgreSQL checkpoints, rendering and publication have named owners. The dependency manifest is pyproject.toml; news and python -m news use the same CLI. RNZ Politics and Business supply domestic coverage. The original park banner, reading column and card styling are preserved.

Retained database records were not deleted or rewritten. Old executable prototype resumes are explicitly unsupported; their saved evidence and editions remain available. Processing uses private environment settings; GitHub deploys only the deliberately exported static reader.

Retrieval and editorial workflow

  1. Collect six fixed public RSS feeds. There is no web-search query or expanded crawler. Keep publication, update and collection times separately.
  2. Retrieve bounded publisher text, falling back to labeled feed evidence. Analyse every valid unique article before applying personal preferences. Reuse exact analyses for 24 hours; the reader pool spans seven days, not only the last 24 hours.
  3. Generate and review each article independently against retained passages. Factual revision and malformed-response repair are bounded. Persistent unsupported drafts are withheld; malformed review contracts stop the run.
  4. Apply explicit, frozen editorial holds from the source audit before grouping. Preserve the original model analysis. Held sources cannot contribute through a multi-source group or an affected preview baseline/archive.
  5. Use distinctive shared words and past source identities to propose comparisons; the model decides whether reports concern the same event. Conflicting comparisons withhold the affected component.
  6. Assess every event against the versioned reader profile. Relevance is broad fit, not a story budget. Importance, freshness, topics and geography are separate. NZ domestic politics/economy does not need global consequences to qualify.
  7. Review multi-source synthesis, feature up to six useful stories with topic diversity, and keep other relevant stories browsable. Save the edition, private audit and reader.

Exact prompts: article generation and source review, event identity, relevance and importance, multi-source synthesis. Reader preferences include politics/conflict, economics/business, AI/technology, science/environment and NZ/Pacific developments; shopping, routine entertainment/sport and isolated incidents without explanatory value are excluded.

Source-fidelity audit

The reviewers compared every one of the 129 model-ready analyses against its full retained extraction, plus two held RNZ analyses: 131 source checks with disjoint recorded coverage. The combined source and reader audits found eleven factual article issues, one upstream headline/body mismatch and one multi-source systems conflation. These produced fifteen source holds; three were held together after one combined summary blurred two distinct systems. Findings included invented measurement units, an announced change presented as completed, reversed time conditions, an unsupported comparison and a related-link headline imported into testimony.

The first complete candidate also contained a voting-deadline inversion. It was withheld, temporal-direction instructions were strengthened, and analyses were regenerated. The regenerated enrolment article remained held. A five-case prompt check improved from three to four expected verdicts, but one supported attribution control was unnecessarily revised. These are regression examples, not an independent accuracy estimate. No held candidate was deployed.

This audit checks fidelity to retained evidence, capped at 8,000 characters per article. It does not establish that each publisher's reporting is independently true or that omitted page text was reviewed. Extraction can still include embedded related links or resolve to a changed page. These observed limits are why source audits and explicit holds remain necessary.

Publication evidence and privacy boundary

news export emitted only the reader HTML, cleaned banner, published archive pages when present and a strict release manifest. The manifest for this edition hashes to d3f905cea0084d423fb81eea619056fe9e28dc6c232255db4b3f32ccfaf65da3. news verify-release verifies the exact inventory, hashes, supported paths and symlinks without loading private runtime settings.

The Pages workflow deploys this exported subset on main. The linked delivered manifest identifies the public run. news confirm-publication independently compares the retained edition, local release and every delivered file, then transactionally records the publication receipt in PostgreSQL. Build, export and CI success alone do not establish delivery.

Publication checks inspect outgoing files and commits with fully redacted credential scanning plus private-network, hostname, personal-data and private-path review. Detailed evidence, raw articles, model responses, hold reasons and runtime settings remain ignored. The active banner has no location/device metadata; pre-existing historical copies were not rewritten. Credential scanning alone is not a complete privacy proof.

Accepted trade-offs and remaining work

No recurring generation scheduler or retention deletion is installed. Generation must be invoked on a machine with private model/database access; GitHub only deploys. A failed checkpoint group of up to five articles can repeat inference within the unfinished group. Model self-review misses factual errors, and lexical proposals can miss paraphrases or mistake context for an update. Metadata-based analysis reuse for 24 hours can miss silent publisher body changes. Six feeds from five publishers are a starting coverage set. Read state is browser-local. The next review should start with the actual reader and the retained rejection/hold reasons, not add sources before examining quality.
